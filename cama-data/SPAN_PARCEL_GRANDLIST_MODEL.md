## 1. The end-to-end pipeline — three systems towns use, and a fourth, downstream of all of them

From a town's perspective, there are **three systems** they're actually subject to, not VCGI's GIS layer, which is downstream of all three:

1. **CAMA (valuation/assessment record-keeping)**. Town-level, vendor-specific (NEMRC MicroSolve for ~77% of towns; Aumentum/ProVal, Vision, Catalis/AssessPro elsewhere. "CAMA" does not necessarily mean NEMRC's MSOL). This is what [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) documents. CAMA's job is appraisal detail (building characteristics, land lines, valuation math). CAMA does **not** issue SPAN.
2. **Grand List (billing & SPAN issuance)**. A separate NEMRC product ("Grand List module") that is used essentially **statewide regardless of which CAMA vendor a town uses**. This is the system that actually generates and maintains SPAN, produces the annual lodged Grand List, and is the record a town submits to the state. **Even towns running Aumentum/Vision/Catalis for CAMA still rely on NEMRC's Grand List module for SPAN.** This is important to how [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) should be read: NEMRC's role in Vermont property administration is broader than "CAMA vendor for 77% of towns". They are also the de facto statewide SPAN-issuing authority, independent of CAMA market share.

   **This does not mean every non-NEMRC CAMA product exports a SPAN-equivalent field, though.** Of the two non-NEMRC CAMA samples examined so far: Aumentum ProVal's extract carries a SPAN-equivalent field, just under a different name (`tax_bill_id` — [PROVAL_AS_BUILT.md](PROVAL_AS_BUILT.md) §2), while Catalis AssessPro's sample carries **no SPAN-equivalent field at all** in any of its three identifier fields ([ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md](ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md) §3). That sample happens to be in a generic third-party export format (Banker & Tradesman) that predates and isn't specific to Vermont's SPAN system, so the absence may reflect the export format's limits rather than AssessPro's actual database. Whether AssessPro's CAMA product tracks a SPAN internally at all, sourced from NEMRC the same way ProVal's does, is unconfirmed. See [ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md](ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md) §6.
3. **VTPIE (Vermont Property Information Exchange)**. A Tax-Department-led platform towns also use, covering select tax-program activities (detail in §1.2).

**VCGI's statewide parcel GIS pipeline is downstream of all three. It is not a fourth system towns are subject to in the same way.** It aggregates whatever parcel geometry a town chooses to submit and joins it to the Grand List via SPAN, publishing the result as the standardized statewide parcel dataset. **Town participation — specifically, submitting updated, SPAN-attributed parcel geometry — remains voluntary**, unlike CAMA, the Grand List, and VTPIE, which are effectively required. Despite that, this published dataset remains the easiest and most useful way for the public to access property information across the state. That gap (a high-value, high-dependency public resource built on voluntary submission of data VCGI doesn't itself originate) is a central reason VCGI undertook this documentation and modernization effort, which has had to go beyond passively receiving whatever geometry towns choose to submit, as-is.

NEMRC's much broader municipal-software footprint beyond CAMA/Grand List (§1.3) and a more precise account of how data actually routes between all of these (§1.1) follow below.

MSOL's `parc_span` field (documented in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) §3) is simply **whatever SPAN the town's Grand List module assigned**. CAMA stores it, but doesn't generate or govern it.

*Source: [`20260518_Parcel_Definition_Workgroup_Source_of_SPAN_Diagram.pdf`](<reference/20260518_Parcel_Definition_Workgroup_Source_of_SPAN_Diagram.pdf>) (in `reference/`) — titled, verbatim, **"AS BUILT?"** by its own authors, with an explicit "?" also placed over the VTPIE box. Treat this as VCGI's own current-best-understanding working model of the existing pipeline, flagged by the workgroup itself as provisional, not a fully confirmed ground truth beyond what's independently corroborated elsewhere in this document (e.g. the real `JoinGL2Parcels` SQL in §4).*

The diagram lays out three tiers: **Town & Vendors → State → Public**, with SPAN originating at the leftmost point ("SOURCE OF SPAN"):

- **NEMRC dB** (the Grand List module's own database) has a **bidirectional** relationship with the **CAMA dB's** (the four vendor CAMA databases — NEMRC MicroSolve, Aumentum/ProVal, Vision, Catalis/AssessPro). The diagram draws this as a two-way arrow, not a one-way "CAMA just reads SPAN from NEMRC" relationship. **For the NEMRC↔MicroSolve pair specifically, this is now well documented (§1.5):** NEMRC pushes identifying/situs data (owner, address, 911 data, tax map, sale/transfer info) into MicroSolve, and MicroSolve pushes valuation totals (Real/Homestead/Housesite) back into NEMRC, automatically, field by field, for parcels that already exist in both systems. **Still unconfirmed:** whether *new*-parcel/SPAN requests ever originate in CAMA rather than the Grand List module (the documented sync only covers value updates on already-existing, already-linked parcels), and whether the other three CAMA vendors (Aumentum/ProVal, Vision, Catalis/AssessPro) have any comparable live sync with NEMRC at all, or rely entirely on the periodic batch extract described in §1.4. Each **CAMA (Vendors)** front-end application separately reads/writes its own **CAMA dB** — the ordinary app-to-its-own-database relationship, not otherwise notable.
- Two **separate extract channels** leave the Town & Vendors tier, and they do **not** travel the same path to the State:
  - **Map Layers (Vendors)** — parcel geometry (Active *and* Inactive layers) produced by GIS/mapping vendors hired by towns (a *different* vendor relationship than the CAMA vendor relationship) — sent to the State on a rolling, per-town basis, not synced to the annual Grand List cycle.
  - **Grand List, other files** — CSV reports generated by NEMRC's Grand List module (fed directly from **NEMRC dB**, per the diagram's own arrow), one of which is the annual Grand List — sent to the **Vermont Department of Taxes**, not directly to VCGI. An example of this channel — the full file family, official field schemas, and a critical finding about inactive-parcel data coverage — is documented in [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md).
- At the **State** tier: the Map Layers extract becomes "Active" Parcels + "Inactive" Parcels + the Intersection Table, all governed by the **GIS Data Standard** (§4). Separately, the Grand List extract becomes the Tax Department's own **"Active" GL** table.
- **VCGI obtains the annual Grand List extract *from the Tax Department*** (not directly from NEMRC or towns) and joins it with **the best available parcel geometry received from each town on a rolling basis** — meaning the Grand List year and the geometry vintage being joined for any given town are not guaranteed to match. This join (formally, the `JoinGL2Parcels` stored procedure documented in §4) combines the GIS-side artifacts with the Tax-Dept-sourced Grand List into the final product: **Statewide Standardized Parcels**, published from the **VCGI dB** to the public.

### 1.2 VTPIE in detail

[VTPIE (Vermont Property Information Exchange)](https://tax.vermont.gov/municipal-officials/vtpie) is a Tax-Department-led platform, distinct from CAMA and the Grand List module (item 3 in §1 above). It handles only select parts of the State's tax-program-related activities:

- Sales Ratio and Equalization Study
- Current Use Processing and Grievances
- Collecting Utility Inventories
- Homestead and Lister Response
- Exemption Management

VTPIE **likely also depends on NEMRC for SPAN**. This is consistent with NEMRC's role as statewide SPAN authority (item 2 above), but this is **explicitly unconfirmed**: the source diagram itself marks VTPIE with a "?" rather than asserting the relationship. Worth direct confirmation (§7).

**The VTPIE↔NEMRC data flow is confirmed for the annual filing cycle** — see [§1.7](#17-the-annual-august-15-filing-workflow-vtpienemrc-411-reconciliation) for the full mechanism. In short: it is bidirectional, not just a one-way upload. NEMRC's As-Billed Grand List is uploaded *into* VTPIE; Current Use (and, on some cadence, Homestead Declaration) data flows back *out* of VTPIE into NEMRC via named, stepped import functions in the Grand List module itself; and **VTPIE independently recalculates its own "411" summary** from the uploaded data, which the town must reconcile against NEMRC's own "Form 411" report before the statutory filing is complete. That last point is a significant fact: VTPIE is not a passive receiving inbox for Grand List data, it's a third system with its own calculation engine capable of reproducing (and sometimes diverging from) the same totals NEMRC computes.

### 1.3 NEMRC's broader municipal software footprint

**Confirmed** per a 2024 screenshot of NEMRC's own "Cloud Services" hosted application launcher. The launcher shows every potential module NEMRC could host for a town, each a distinct product: 
- **AP** (Accounts Payable),
- **AR** (Accounts Receivable),
- **DG** (Animal/Dog Licenses),
- **CAMA** (MicroSolve, branded simply "CAMA" in the launcher),
- **CR** (Cash Receipts),
- **GL** (General Ledger),
- **LS** (Grand List — the SPAN-issuing module this whole document is about),
- **LR** (Land Records),
- **PR** (Payroll),
- **TA** (Tax Administration),
- **UB** (Utility Billing),
- **VT** (Voter Checklist).

Two of these are worth flagging:

- **Land Records (LR)** — a separate NEMRC product from the Grand List module. Plausible, but **not directly confirmed**: for towns using NEMRC's own LR product (rather than a separate land-records/deed-recording vendor), this could be the actual point of origin for the sale/transfer data (book, page, sale date, price, validity) already confirmed flowing NEMRC → MSOL ([§1.5](#15-the-grand-list-modules-own-parcelcontiguous-parcel-ui)) — i.e. Land Records → Grand List → CAMA, all within one vendor's ecosystem, for towns using all three NEMRC products. Worth confirming directly (§7).
- **Tax Administration (TA)** — a separate module from Grand List, and directly confirmed as a real destination: the Grand List module's own main menu has a dedicated **"Transfer to Tax Admin"** function ([§1.5](#15-the-grand-list-modules-own-parcelcontiguous-parcel-ui)), giving a defined mechanism behind the previously-vague "Tax Billing and Municipal Tax Rates" description.

NEMRC's centrality to Vermont municipal tax administration isn't just "CAMA vendor for 77% of towns, and separately the SPAN-issuing authority", it's infrastructural across most of the municipal administration (accounting, payroll, utility billing, voter records, and potentially land records, alongside Grand List and CAMA). That's a different negotiating position than a single-product CAMA vendor relationship, and worth factoring into how any schema-change ask (§6) is prioritized or sequenced with NEMRC.

### 1.4 The CAMA extract submission channel: 32 V.S.A. § 5404(b) and Globalscape FTP

This is the statutory basis for the CAMA-documentation effort in this repo, and the mechanism by which the MSOL sample data ([MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md)) physically reached VCGI.

**The statute:** annually, on or before **August 15**, the clerk of a municipality (or the supervisor of an unorganized town or gore) must transmit to the Director of Property Valuation and Review (PVR) "an extract of the assessor database also referred to as a Computer Assisted Mass Appraisal (CAMA) system or Computer Assisted Mass Appraisal database," identifying each parcel by "a parcel identification number assigned under a numbering system prescribed by the Director." **That numbering system is SPAN**, and per VCGI's understanding it is expected to remain SPAN through the Act 164/H.933 parcel-definition redesign (§6) — whatever new relational structure (`ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN`/etc.) emerges, SPAN itself continues to be the Director-prescribed parcel identifier for §5404(b) purposes. Per the Tax Department's own policy advisor, this specific subsection was enacted by **Act 69 of 2025 (S.127), Sec. 6, effective July 1, 2025** — it is already in force, and **statewide compliance has not yet been achieved.**

**No prescribed electronic transfer method exists.** In practice, CAMA vendors — not towns directly, despite the statute naming the municipal clerk/supervisor as the transmitting party — build tailored reports for Vermont's specific request (as they already do for other states they operate in) and submit on the town's behalf.

**NEMRC's data transfer mechanism, confirmed working today:** an existing State of Vermont secure FTP system called **Globalscape**. Per Tax Department IT staff describing the setup: NEMRC logs into their own Globalscape account and sees a folder named `CAMA-NEMRC`, into which they place CAMA data intended for the state; that folder is virtually tied to VCGI's own `CAMA\NEMRC` folder on the state's side, so anything NEMRC uploads appears almost instantaneously on VCGI's side without any separate transfer step. **This is how both MSOL samples documented in this repo (South Burlington and Lincoln) reached VCGI.**

**Unresolved:** whether Aumentum/ProVal, Vision, and Catalis/AssessPro are expected to use the same Globalscape system (in their own vendor-specific folders, mirroring NEMRC's arrangement) or a separate transfer mechanism entirely; unconfirmed as of this writing. An API-based transfer is considered unlikely by VCGI. This is an open question, not a hypothetical: [ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md](ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md) §3 already found no SPAN-equivalent field in the one Catalis sample examined. If that holds for whatever Catalis actually submits under §5404(b), it would be a direct statutory-compliance gap (an extract that doesn't identify parcels by the Director-prescribed numbering system), not just a data-completeness quirk. Worth raising directly with Catalis and the Tax Department, not just NEMRC.

### 1.5 The Grand List module's own parcel/contiguous-parcel UI

*Source: three screenshots of NEMRC's Grand List module "Parcel Maintenance" screen ([01](<reference/NEMRC Inactive Current Practice 01.png>), [02](<reference/NEMRC Inactive Current Practice 02.png>), [03](<reference/NEMRC Inactive Current Practice 03.png>)), two vendor training documents ([`NEMRC Parcel transfers-splits-editing.pdf`](<reference/NEMRC Parcel transfers-splits-editing.pdf>), 8/12/2016; [`NEMRC-Inactive Parcels Recording Practice Notes 20190409.pdf`](<reference/NEMRC-Inactive Parcels Recording Practice Notes 20190409.pdf>)), a third NEMRC training document, **"Link between NEMRC and CAMA"** (undated; its own embedded screenshot shows a parcel "last updated on 01/27/2014," giving a rough age), and four 2024 screenshots of NEMRC's own "Cloud Services" hosted-application demo (application launcher, Grand List Entity Menu, Grand List main menu, and Reports Menu, all using the "Town of Anytown" demo login, version 9.3m) — all provided directly, not currently stored in this repo. All are NEMRC's own materials, not the MSOL CAMA software documented in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md), but the separate NEMRC Grand List module described in §1 item 2. All use a generic "ANYTOWN"/"Anytown Grand List" demo dataset, the same convention already identified in the MSOL Lincoln sample ([MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) §9) — reinforcing that this is NEMRC's standard cross-product training/demo dataset, not real town data, and that the Parcel Maintenance screen's layout (Parcel Status, Tax status, Category, Owner, Cama file, "Contiguous Parcel Information") has been stable across training materials spanning roughly a decade, into the actively-hosted 2024 Cloud Services product.*

This is the first direct look at how the Grand List module (not CAMA, not the GIS layer) actually implements the Active/Inactive/contiguous-parcel mechanism already discussed abstractly in §4 above — and it resolves, extends, or complicates several things documented so far:

**The mechanism, confirmed at the software level.** The Grand List module's "Parcel Maintenance" screen has a `Parcel Status` field (`Active`/`Inactive`) and, **only visible/populated on inactive records**, a "Contiguous Parcel Information" panel showing the **Parcel # (not SPAN)** of the active parcel it's combined with, plus that active parcel's owner/status/acreage for the lister's reference. This is a one-directional pointer (inactive → active); the active parcel's own record doesn't have a matching field to fill in — instead, its `.Contig` tab shows a **computed rollup** listing every inactive parcel currently pointing at it. A real (demo) example confirms this end-to-end: active parcel `03040127.100` (Beacon Pest Control & Chimney Control, 93.90 total acres) has three inactive child parcels (`.110`, `.120`, `.130`, 18.80/19.40/19.40 acres) rolled into it, visible both as a printed Grand List report and in the live `.Contig` tab.

**Important correction to how `ADMINSPAN` (§6) should be understood relative to current practice:** the existing linkage is **`Parcel #`-keyed, not SPAN-keyed** — confirmed directly on the inactive parcel `03040127.110` (owner "Hallock Clara Et Al"), which has its **own independent SPAN** (`354-109-10817`) as a static attribute, while its "Contiguous Parcel Information" panel points at the active parcel using `Parcel # 03040127-100`, not that parcel's SPAN. This matches the Parcel Data Standard's own statement that "both active and inactive parcels have SPAN numbers" (§4) — each keeps one — but means the proposed `ADMINSPAN` field, if implemented as a SPAN-formatted cross-reference, would be a schema change from how NEMRC's Grand List module tracks this relationship today, not simply a renaming of an existing SPAN-keyed field.

**The "what syncs between Grand List and CAMA" question (§7 item 8) is substantially answered — with two distinct behaviors for two distinct transaction types, not a single contradictory pattern.**

**For parcel splits/transfers specifically** — per NEMRC's own 2016 training document, when a lister completes a split/transfer and adjusts an active parcel's acreage accordingly, the instructions explicitly state: *"You will have to adjust the acreage in your appraisal program manually and generate a new value for this parcel as well."* **There is no automatic sync for this transaction type — it's manual, dual entry performed by the same lister in both systems.** This makes sense given the mechanism below: a split/transfer changes *which parcels exist* (new SPANs, new records), and the ongoing field-level sync only applies to parcels the two systems already agree exist.

**For ordinary value/identifying-data updates on an already-existing, already-synced parcel**, per the "Link between NEMRC and CAMA" document, the two systems **do** sync automatically, field by field, in both directions:

- **NEMRC → MSOL (identifying/situs data):** owner name, mailing address (city/state/zip), **911 data**, tax map number, property description, total acres, and sale/transfer data (sale date, sale price, book, page, validity code).
- **MSOL → NEMRC (valuation data):** MSOL's `cama_total` field → NEMRC's "Real" value; `homestd_va` → NEMRC's Homestead value; `cama_site2` → NEMRC's Housesite value (field names per [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md §3](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md#3-the-parcel-level-parent-table-exp_main) — this document didn't name MSOL's internal fields, but they match its "CAMA Total"/"Homestead"/"Housesite" concepts exactly).
- **An automatic "Change of Appraisal" flag**: when a CAMA-sourced value differs from NEMRC's own stored "previous" value for Real/Homestead/Housesite, NEMRC automatically flags that parcel/value as changed for that year — likely the same mechanism behind the "Change Of Appraisal notices" already referenced in the Grievance section of [VTPIE_CAMA_REQUIREMENTS_PARCEL_ANALYSIS.md](VTPIE/VTPIE_CAMA_REQUIREMENTS_PARCEL_ANALYSIS.md).
- **The mechanics, confirmed by name:** NEMRC's own live parcel database is called `LSPROP01`; MSOL's own live parcel table is called `MAIN` — directly matching the `table_id="MAIN"` value already independently confirmed in MSOL's own `EXP_DATADICT` metadata ([MSOL_AS_BUILT.md §5](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md#5-the-self-describing-schema-datadict--categ-and-why-it-matters)). Sync requires both programs to have live file access to these tables — it silently fails if either file is locked (e.g., a user left the parcel open in both programs, or antivirus/firewall software is scanning the files).
- **Built-in verification:** running the Grand List module's own "1 Grand List" report checks whether all appropriate data has transferred between the two programs and prints a list of any out-of-sync parcels. **A second, more direct confirmation of this same sync-checking concern:** a 2024 screenshot of the Grand List module's own main menu (Town of Anytown demo, version 9.3m) shows a dedicated **"Check Sync with CAMA"** button sitting directly on the home screen, alongside the "Mass Appraisal Values Supplied By MICROSOLVE" banner — i.e., checking CAMA sync status is treated as routine enough to warrant a permanent, one-click button on the module's own front page, not just a report buried in a menu.
- **Manual reconciliation ("forcing a change") when sync fails:** make a small, deliberate edit to the out-of-sync field in NEMRC (e.g., appending characters to an owner name) and save; confirm the change appears in CAMA; then correct it back in NEMRC. For values, make and then remove a small adjustment in CAMA, re-running cost each time, to force a fresh value to push through. If that fails, close and reopen both programs; in rare cases, a full computer reboot is needed to release the file locks.

**This is the single most complete answer available anywhere in this documentation set to what flows between CAMA and the Grand List module** — an advance over the historical (2003, [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) intro) description of the same basic pattern, which named the same three synced values (Real/Homestead/Housesite) without this level of mechanical detail. **Caveat: this document is undated but appears to describe a ~2014-era (or earlier) desktop-software architecture** (file-level locking, no mention of any web/API layer) — whether this exact mechanism is unchanged today is worth confirming directly with NEMRC, though the fact that the 2003 and ~2014 sources describe the same fields syncing the same way is a meaningful sign of stability, not churn. **Confirmed still current as of 2024**, per the same Cloud Services screenshot cited above (version 9.3m, dated 03/16/2020 install with a live 2024 demo session) — the "Check Sync with CAMA" button and the overall menu structure described below both come from this modern, actively-hosted version, not a legacy one.

**A previously undocumented architectural fact: the Grand List module maintains multiple numbered "Entities" per town, not just one live database.** A 2024 screenshot of the module's own Entity Menu (demo login) shows, for a single town, one live/current entity ("1. Town of Anytown") alongside four separate, distinctly-labeled snapshot entities: "6. Anytown 2016 Billed," "7. Anytown 2017 Billed," "8. Anytown 2018 Billed," "9. Anytown 2019 Billed" (entities 2–5 weren't visible in the screenshot provided, so their contents are unknown). This means NEMRC's historical Grand List preservation mechanism isn't just "prior value" fields on the live record ([MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md)'s `EXP_OYVAL` is the CAMA-side analog) — it's **full, separate, frozen copies of the entire town database, one per completed billing year**, that a user can open directly like any other entity. The main menu's own item **"6. Make New Entity"** is the likely mechanism for creating these, and item **"3. Annual Rollover"** is now **confirmed** (per a Tax Department town-filing instruction sheet, [§1.7](#17-the-annual-august-15-filing-workflow-vtpienemrc-411-reconciliation)) to be what freezes the current year into a new "as billed" entity and advances the live one. **Why this matters for the redesign:** if a specific past year's Grand List is needed to match a specific vintage of parcel geometry (the timing-mismatch problem already flagged — [§7](#7-open-questions-for-the-ongoing-workgroup) item 10), a frozen "Billed" entity for that exact year might be a cleaner, more literal historical source than reconstructing it from the live entity's own prior-value fields — worth asking NEMRC directly whether these frozen entities are queryable/exportable on demand, or purely an internal reporting mechanism. The Reports Menu's own **"O. Compare other Grand List"** item is plausibly the tool built for comparing across these entities (e.g., live vs. a frozen year, or one town vs. another) — again inference, not confirmed.

State Treasurers Report"** (a previously unconfirmed state-reporting touchpoint, purpose unknown).

**A real-world worked example matching the proposed model's "horizontal" diagrams exactly** (§6.2) — one active parcel with a collective tax bill, several inactive sub-parcels each retaining their own SPAN and acreage, all owned in common. The 2016 training document's own definition — *"Contiguous parcels – are defined as parcels that touch each other and the ownership is in the same name. When this happens both parcels must be combined together and taxed as one parcel"* — independently corroborates the Parcel Data Standard's Active/Inactive definition (§4) from the vendor's own user-facing documentation, in almost identical language.

**A new edge case, not previously covered:** the 2019 practice-notes document instructs listers to check *"Is the parcel near the town border? Does the Grantee own property in the adjoining town in which this parcel would be contiguous"* — i.e., **contiguous-parcel combination can be relevant across town lines.** This sits awkwardly with everything else documented here: each town runs its own separate NEMRC Grand List database (§1 item 2), and SPAN's own town-code segment (§2) is town-specific, so it's architecturally unclear whether — or how — an active/inactive pairing could actually span two different towns' separate databases. More likely, this guidance is about the lister correctly recognizing legal/practical contiguity for valuation-judgment purposes, while the software's actual Active/Inactive combination remains scoped within one town's own SPAN range — but this is inference, not confirmed. Worth a direct question (§7).

**A second, distinct cross-town condition — one physical parcel straddling a town line, not two separate parcels being combined — is now confirmed, not just inferred.** Per a NEMRC CAMA training handout, "Land in Two Towns" (2/21/2024, not in this repo): when a single deeded parcel's boundary crosses a town line, **each town independently assesses only its own portion**, with no formal system linking the two towns' separate records — the neighboring town's acreage is referenced *only* as an input to the local town's own land-valuation formula (to get the right acreage-tiered rate), never as "the same underlying parcel, administered elsewhere." Full mechanism and a documented data-entry pitfall (mismatched land-type codes silently drop the cross-town acreage from the calculation) in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md §10](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md#10-land-valuation-tables-and-the-land-in-two-towns-cross-town-blending-method). Per the user's own account: this — like the still-open question above about cross-town contiguous parcels — traces back to the same root cause, that Vermont has never completed a definitive, modern, statewide survey of municipal corner points and boundaries (see [readme.md](readme.md)'s "Why Modernize" section) — unlike, e.g., Massachusetts. **This has a real bearing on the Act 164 parcel-definition redesign, addressed in §5 and §7 below**, since it means the entire current pipeline — not just SPAN, not just CAMA — has no mechanism for representing a single physical parcel that spans two towns as one entity.

**Other tabs visible on the Parcel Maintenance screen, confirming/extending §1.3:** `TIF` (Tax Increment Financing — confirms this is a tab within the same parcel record, not a separate disconnected product, tightening the bullet-point description in §1.3) and `HS-122` (the Vermont Homestead Declaration form number). **This is a useful data point that had only been speculated about earlier in this documentation effort** (an earlier working assumption was that homestead declarations are administered through some separate, standalone state system rather than CAMA) — more precisely, per this screenshot evidence, it's the **Grand List module itself** (not a separate standalone system, and not CAMA) that natively manages homestead declarations at the parcel level, via a dedicated tab on the same parcel record that also carries SPAN, acreage, and Active/Inactive status. Relevant to how a future dwelling-use attestation (Act 170) might be implemented — likely as an extension of this same existing tab/workflow rather than an entirely new system.

**A data-freshness caveat worth flagging, not asserting as a bug:** the same inactive parcel (`03040127.110`) shows owner "Hallock Clara Et Al," while the active parcel it's combined into shows a different, more recent owner ("Beacon Pest Control & Chimney Control"). Since the Parcel Data Standard's Active/Inactive definition requires common ownership at the time of combination, this is most likely a case where the inactive parcel's own owner field was never updated after a later ownership change (it no longer drives billing, so there may be no operational reason to keep it current) rather than a data error — but it's an illustration that owner fields on long-inactive parcels shouldn't be assumed current.

### 1.6 Town-optional local tax-map integration (out of scope for the statewide pipeline, but worth a few cross-confirmations)

*Source: a NEMRC training seminar document, "MSOL_2018_Seminar_Morning.pdf" (dated 2/9/2018 on its own slides), specifically its "Linking Cama Data to Tax Maps — What are your options" section — provided directly, not currently stored in this repo.*

**This describes a different thing than the rest of §1** — a town's own, entirely optional decision to embed CAMA/Grand List data into its own **local digital tax map** (hosted publicly or only within the town office), so that clicking a parcel on that map shows owner info, values, sketches, and photos. This is keyed to whatever **local identifiers** the town's own map already uses (Parcel ID, Map ID/tax map number) — not part of the statewide, SPAN-keyed pipeline this document otherwise covers, and not a statutory requirement. Noted here only because a few incidental details corroborate or extend findings already documented elsewhere:

- **The town's own "tax mapper" is directly involved in this integration** ("Discuss with your tax mapper that this is something you would like to do. They will likely give you some input on file layouts") — consistent with, and a real-world instance of, the separate "Map Layers (Vendors)" GIS/mapping-vendor relationship already documented in §1.1 as distinct from the CAMA vendor relationship.
- **NEMRC's Grand List module Reports Menu is visible in full for the first time**, and two entries are worth noting: **"4. Form 411"** — direct, additional confirmation that "Form 411" is simply a named report within the Grand List module itself, consistent with the "411 = colloquial name for the Grand List" finding in [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md §1](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md#1-what-this-is-and-why-its-a-different-channel-than-the-cama-extract); and **"10. Contiguous Parcels"** plus a separate **"Inactive Parcels"** report — mild supporting evidence (not proof) that the Grand List module can report on active/inactive status internally, relevant to the still-open question of whether the TIF-only inactive-status export gap ([§5](#5-limits-of-the-current-model-pre-redesign) item 7) is a Tax-Department export-scoping choice rather than a software limitation.
- **A new, distinct confidentiality data point:** NEMRC's own guidance explicitly warns "some of that information is confidential such as **911 numbers and state payments**. So you would not want to include that information" on a public tax map. The 911-address point is consistent with the Safe At Home discussion already documented ([VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md §4.2](VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md#42-ownership--address-fields-phase-1-except-where-noted)) — though note this reads as a blanket "911 data is sensitive" caution from NEMRC, not necessarily scoped only to Safe-At-Home-flagged owners. **"State payments" is new and unexplained** — some category of state-payment data apparently lives in the Grand List record and is treated as confidential, distinct from anything else documented so far. Worth asking NEMRC/Tax Dept directly what this refers to.
- **Two separate, ad hoc, user-configurable export mechanisms are shown, and neither is the standardized statewide extract already documented elsewhere.** NEMRC's own "H. Export Data" (Reports Menu) lets a lister pick specific Grand List fields to export; MSOL CAMA has its own, separate generic "Export" tool (`Tools → Export`) for picking specific CAMA fields. Both are for a town's own ad hoc purposes (like this tax-map integration) — **neither is the fixed-schema `EXP_*`/411 file family** used for the standardized §5404(b) CAMA extract or the annual Grand List submission to the Tax Department. Worth keeping these mechanisms distinct in any future conversation with NEMRC, since they're easy to conflate.
- **A local "sub-parcel" numbering convention, unrelated to SPAN:** the worked example shows a parcel labeled `5:59,b` (map:lot,sub-parcel-letter), with fields `Is Sub Parcel: T`, `Sub Parcel Char: b`, `Sub Parcel Count: 2` — the town's own map vendor tracking a lot split (`5:59,a` / `5:59,b`) using its own local convention, entirely separate from SPAN or the proposed `ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN` fields. An interesting real-world data point that local map-ID schemes already have their own informal way of representing what Act 164's Parcel/Administrative-Parcel split is trying to formalize statewide — but not itself part of that statewide model.

### 1.7 The annual August 15 filing workflow: VTPIE↔NEMRC 411 reconciliation

*Source: "Filing Your August 15, 2026, As Billed Grand List with the State of Vermont" (Vermont Department of Taxes, Property Valuation and Review, revised July 2026, **NTC-1362**), in [`reference/`](reference/Filing-Aug-15-2026-As-Billed-Grand-List-NTC-1362.pdf) — a town-facing instruction sheet for the statutory filing already documented in [§1.4](#14-the-cama-extract-submission-channel-32-vsa--5404b-and-globalscape-ftp). This is the first direct, procedural look at how VTPIE and NEMRC's Grand List module actually interoperate today, and it resolves or extends several things documented elsewhere in this section.*

**This is a different statutory channel than §1.4** — §1.4 is the CAMA-extract requirement (32 V.S.A. §5404(b), submitted via Globalscape). This is the Grand List's own annual submission — the "411" file family already documented in [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md) — and it turns out to route *through VTPIE*, not straight from NEMRC to the Tax Department.

**The confirmed mechanism, in sequence:**

1. The town uploads its **2026 As Billed** NEMRC Grand List into VTPIE, using a **"Certified to Clerk"** file type — the first confirmed example of a named VTPIE upload/file-type taxonomy.
2. VTPIE processes the upload, and the lister works through **Current Use reverification directly in VTPIE** (Tax Programs → Current Use).
3. Current Use data (and, on a separate cadence, Homestead Declaration data — the instructions require confirming homesteads have been "downloaded... for that week" as a precondition) flows **back out of VTPIE into NEMRC**, via a named, stepped import function in the Grand List module's own main menu: **"9. State Current Use Maintenance" → "Step 1: Get Current Use data from VTPIE" → "Step 3: Load Grand List with Current Use Values."** This directly confirms what that menu item (visible but unexplained in the 2024 Cloud Services screenshots reviewed previously) actually does.
4. The town generates a **"Form 411"** directly in NEMRC (Reports → 4. Form 411) *and* a separate **"Grand List 411 Summary"** generated independently *inside VTPIE itself*, from whatever has been uploaded/reconciled there — **VTPIE recalculates its own totals; it does not simply echo back what NEMRC sent it.**
5. The two independently-computed 411s must match before the town submits its **August 15** filing — done by printing NEMRC's 411 electronically ("Submit your 411 electronically to the state") and then uploading the As-Billed Grand List into VTPIE a *second* time, using a distinct file type: **"Final As Billed Values (August Submission to PVR, use this only once)."**
6. If a **District Advisor (DA)** — a Tax Department support role not previously documented — is needed, they're the town's point of contact throughout.

**Confirms, rather than merely infers, the "Entity" snapshot mechanism from §1.5:** the instructions explicitly distinguish the town's still-open "working grand list" from its frozen **"as billed" entity**, and say to use the as-billed entity for this filing "after you have rolled over and created" it — **directly confirming that "Annual Rollover" is what creates the as-billed entity**, resolving what §1.5 previously flagged as "a strong, reasonable inference... not directly confirmed."

**A statutorily-current confirmation of the `TC` (Telecommunications) category** already flagged in [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md §6](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md#6-the-16-category-property-classification-system): *"For 2026 there was a statutory update where PP Cable became Real Property and is categorized as Telecommunications Property (TC)... There is also no Exemption for Cable anymore, so the exemption should be removed completely."*

**A data-quality gotcha, worth flagging directly:** the instructions themselves note *"the total listed values on the NEMRC 411 are the total Listed Value divided by 100, whereas on the VTPIE 411 Total Listed values are not."* **This is confirmed only for the two printed/displayed "411" report outputs** (NEMRC's own Form 411 report and VTPIE's independently-generated summary) — whether this same ×100 scaling discrepancy also affects the raw `411_gl57.csv`/`411TOT57.csv` files VCGI's own pipeline actually consumes is **unconfirmed and worth checking directly**, since a silent 100x error in any value field would be a serious, load-bearing data-quality risk if unaccounted for.

**Other reconciliation-checklist items worth noting as real, current business rules:** Veterans exemption counts are reconciled against an annual **"Final Approved Veterans List" issued by VOVA** (Vermont Office of Veterans' Affairs) each May — a Tax-Dept-adjacent external party not previously documented in this repo. **"Voted Grandfathered" status is restricted to Fire, Rescue & Ambulance properties only** — a specific, narrow business rule. A negative Partial Statutory exemption value (with no parcel count) indicates a statutorily-exempt parcel whose prior-year exemption now exceeds its current value — a real, documented VTPIE data-quality edge case.

**Bearing on the Act 164/170 parcel redefinition, per direct request:** this entire annual reconciliation is built around comparing two independently-computed sets of totals — including a **"Parcel Count" line** already reconciled between NEMRC's and VTPIE's 411s today. Once `PARCLCOUNT` (the new sellable-lot count) exists alongside today's administrative parcel count, **this reconciliation process would need to either add a second count to compare, or make clear which of the two counts the existing "Parcel Count" line already represents** — this isn't automatic, and isn't addressed anywhere in the current proposal. More broadly: **VTPIE is a third system with its own independent calculation engine**, not just NEMRC/CAMA — any new field the redesign adds (`DWELLINGS`, the three-way classification, `KIND`/`TYPE`) that's expected to appear on the statutory Grand List filing would need to be independently computable and reconcilable in VTPIE too, not just added to NEMRC's own schema. Worth raising directly with the Tax Department (§7).

## 2. SPAN: structure and authority

**SPAN = School Property Account Number.** Per the Parcel Data Standard's own definition: *"a unique, state-assigned identification number for each parcel/unlanded building. SPAN number information is critical to database synergy between Vermont GIS parcel data and Grand List data."*

Format: `XXX-YYY-ZZZZZ` (13 characters including dashes) —

- **XXX** — 3-digit town code. Valid values are enumerated in the **`PT_TOWN`** field (Department of Taxes-maintained) of the [VT Data - Geographic Area Names and Codes](https://services1.arcgis.com/BkFxaEFNwHqX3tAw/arcgis/rest/services/FS_VCGI_OPENDATA_TABLE_GEOCODES_COMMCODE_v1/FeatureServer/0) table (the "commcode" table), itself governed by the [Vermont Geographic Area Names and Codes Data Standard](https://files.vcgi.vermont.gov/other/standards-guidelines/geonames-codes/geonames-codes-standard.html).
- **YYY** — 3-digit **school district code** (explaining the name "School Property Account Number" — the SPAN encodes school-district affiliation, which matters for education-property-tax apportionment). Values correspond to the same table's **`AOE_CODE`** field (Agency of Education-maintained, "code denoting municipalities within Agency of Education data"), stripped of its leading `T` character (e.g. `AOE_CODE` of `T036` → SPAN segment `036`).
- **ZZZZZ** — 5-digit unique sequence number, generated and maintained by **NEMRC's Grand List module**.

Two named examples from the MSOL samples documented in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md): South Burlington's town code is `600`; Lincoln's is `354` (hardcoded as "Lincoln (354)" in that sample's own demo-app documentation).

**Important nuance on XXX/YYY:** this doesn't mean NEMRC relies on `PT_TOWN`/`AOE_CODE` values to *generate* a SPAN — it means VCGI's own Geographic Area Names and Codes Standard has documented these two fields specifically to reflect how SPAN's first two segments are actually used in practice. The crosswalk table is VCGI's reference for what those digits *correspond to*, not NEMRC's generation mechanism.

SPAN join comparisons in production are done **with dashes stripped** (`REPLACE(span,'-','')`) — worth remembering if building any SPAN-matching logic, since formatting isn't perfectly consistent source to source.

## 4. Current GIS parcel data model (Vermont GIS Parcel Data Standard v2.3, Oct. 2016)

Full standard: [html](https://vcgitimterway.github.io/claude-code-demo-day-2/examples/parcel/parcel-standard.html) / [raw](https://github.com/VCGITimTerway/claude-code-demo-day-2/blob/main/examples/parcel/parcel-standard.html). Two compliance levels — Level 1 (minimum) and Level 2 (adds easements). Each town delivers a `.gdb` or shapefile set named `VTPARCELS_<town>_<YYYY>.gdb`.

**Feature classes / tables required at Level 1:**

| Name | Geometry | Purpose | Key fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| `poly_parcels` | Polygon | **Active** parcels (excludes unlanded structures), ROWs, water boundaries | `SPAN`, `MAPID`, `PROPTYPE`, `MATCHSTAT`, + edit/source metadata |
| `poly_inactive` | Polygon | Inactive parcels + their related active parcel | `STATUS` (ACTIVE/INACTIVE), `PARENTSPAN`, `SPAN`, `MAPID`, `PROPTYPE`, `MATCHSTAT` |
| `line_parcels` | Line | Boundary lines backing `poly_parcels` | `PLTYPE`, `PARCBOUND` |
| Intersection table | Table | Relates Grand List records to `poly_parcels` | `GIS_SPAN`, `GLIST_SPAN`, `YEAR`, `TOWN` |
| `poly_easements` (Level 2 only) | Polygon | Access/utility/etc. easements | `ESMTTYPE` |

**Note the important field-name distinction the standard itself makes explicit:** the note-worthy fields are on the *intersection table*, not directly on `poly_parcels` — `GIS_SPAN` ("the SPAN exactly as attributed in the SPAN field of `poly_parcels`") and `GLIST_SPAN` ("SPAN exactly as listed in the municipality's Grand List... corresponding to GIS_SPAN"). **Duplicate `GIS_SPAN` entries are explicitly valid** — that's the mechanism that produces "stacked polygons" (one physical footprint, many Grand List records), most commonly for condominiums.

**Active vs. Inactive, as currently defined (this is the definition Act 164/H.933 is superseding — see §5):** *"when two or more abutting parcels have the same owner, one of those parcels is considered the main parcel, or active parcel, to which a single collective tax bill is associated for all of the parcels. The other parcels are inactive... Both active and inactive parcels have SPAN numbers."* `poly_inactive` already carries a `PARENTSPAN` field — the SPAN of the related Active parcel — which is functionally a **precursor to the proposed `ADMINSPAN` field** (§6), just scoped only to the inactive-parcel case rather than being a general-purpose linking field.

**Condominium common land convention (existing today):** when common land has no SPAN of its own, `poly_parcels.SPAN` is populated with a synthetic filler: `C-<FIPS6 town code>-<sequence>` (e.g. `C-7085-1` for Williston). This is an existing, narrower analog to what the proposed `GROUNDSPAN` field (§6) would generalize. VCGI's own [Act 68 of 2024 parcels report](https://github.com/VCGI/publications/blob/main/Act68_2024/Act68-2024-Parcels-VCGI_As_Submitted_20241212.md) (submitted 2024-12-12) already recommended broadening this single `C-` prefix into a two-letter type-specific prefix system — e.g. `CO` (condominium), `MH` (mobile home park), `WT` (wind turbine) — followed by the town code and a sequence (e.g. `CO-003-0001`), for uniform synthetic-SPAN attribution of unlanded structures/common interest parcels in the Intersection Table (§3.3.4 of that report). That report also documents four distinct current mapping approaches for unlanded structures/common interest parcels, useful background for §6's proposed model:

| Method | Description | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Stacked** (current statewide recommendation) | Identical polygons stacked on the same footprint, related via the Intersection Table's `GIS_SPAN`/`GLIST_SPAN` | Simple geometry to maintain; includes common land in total acreage; no visual distinction between unit and common land |
| **Discrete** | Individual building-footprint polygons; common land has **no** SPAN | Visually distinguishes units from common land; avoids GIS/Grand-List SPAN mismatch; time-intensive to build/maintain footprints |
| **Distributed** | Individual building-footprint polygons; common land **does** have its own SPAN | Same trade-offs as Discrete, but common land is independently identified |
| **Points** (used elsewhere, e.g. Dakota County, MN) | One point per tax parcel ID, condos included as points | Some visual distinction without subtracting from total calculated acreage; requires a wholly separate geometry layer; not currently used in VT |

**Published statewide layers** (the aggregate product VCGI actually serves, one level up from the raw per-town delivery — this is what the demo CAMA viewer queries):

- Active: `https://services1.arcgis.com/BkFxaEFNwHqX3tAw/ArcGIS/rest/services/FS_VCGI_OPENDATA_Cadastral_VTPARCELS_poly_standardized_parcels_SP_v1/FeatureServer/0`
- Inactive: `https://services1.arcgis.com/BkFxaEFNwHqX3tAw/ArcGIS/rest/services/FS_VCGI_OPENDATA_Cadastral_VTPARCELS_poly_standardized_inactive_SP_v1/FeatureServer/0`

These are produced by the `JoinGL2Parcels` stored procedure: an intersection-table join (`GLIST_SPAN` → Grand List `span`) followed by a join back to `poly_parcels` geometry (`GIS_SPAN` → `SPAN`, with dashes stripped on both sides), duplicating geometry wherever `GIS_SPAN` appears more than once — that's the actual mechanism producing the stacked-polygon effect referenced throughout this documentation and in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md).

**Both layers' actual field schemas, confirmed directly (retrieved and supplied by the user, not inferred):**

*Active layer* (`poly_standardized_parcels`) — the full `JoinGL2Parcels` output. Beyond the Grand-List-derived fields already itemized in §3, its own GIS-side fields are: `OBJECTID`; `SPAN` (alias "GIS SPAN" — the GIS/mapping vendor's own SPAN attribute, distinct from `GLIST_SPAN`); `MAPID`; `PROPTYPE`; `YEAR` (alias "GIS Year" — distinct from `GLYEAR`); `TOWN` (distinct from `TNAME`); `SOURCENAME`, `SOURCETYPE`, `SOURCEDATE`, `EDITMETHOD`, `EDITOR`, `EDITDATE` (edit/source lineage metadata, previously only gestured at in the feature-class table above as "+ edit/source metadata" — this is the actual field list); `MATCHSTAT`; `EDITNOTE`; plus the standard Esri `Shape__Area`/`Shape__Length` fields. Every field name/alias here is the real, live one.

*Inactive layer* (`poly_standardized_inactive`) — `OBJECTID`, `STATUS` (coded domain: `ACTIVE`, `INACTIVE`), `PARENTSPAN`, `SPAN`, `MAPID`, `PROPTYPE` (coded domain includes `PARCEL`, `ROW_ROAD`, `ROW_RAIL`, +2 more), `YEAR`, `TOWN` (a coded domain enumerating VT towns individually — 255 listed in the schema retrieved, a slightly different count than the ~260 figure used elsewhere in this document for towns/CAMA vendor share, likely reflecting unorganized towns/gores not being separately coded here; not worth over-reading), `SOURCENAME`, `SOURCETYPE` (coded domain, e.g. `SURVEY/PLAT`, `DEED`, `LINES OF OCCUPATION`, +4 more), `SOURCEDATE`, `EDITMETHOD` (coded domain, e.g. `HEADS UP DIGITIZING`, `COORDINATED COGO`, `NON-COORDINATED COGO`, +6 more), `EDITOR`, `EDITDATE`, `MATCHSTAT` (coded domain: `MATCH`, `EXEMPT`, `UNMATCHED`), `EDITNOTE`, `Shape__Area`, `Shape__Length`.

**This confirms §5 item 7's finding at the level of the actual published statewide product, not just one town's TIF sample:** the Inactive layer carries **no Grand-List-derived fields at all** — no owner, no value, no exemption, no category, nothing from §3's table. Every field on it is pure GIS geometry/provenance metadata. Per direct confirmation: this is because **there generally isn't equivalent Grand List information available to join against inactive parcels in the first place** — consistent with [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md) §7's finding that the Tax Department's own NEMRC-sourced data only carries per-parcel Active/Inactive status for TIF-district towns. The Active layer, by contrast, is where all the Grand-List value/ownership/exemption detail lives — meaning today's entire inactive-parcel record, for the ~240 non-TIF towns, is **geometry and provenance only, with no attached valuation or ownership data anywhere in the published statewide product.** This is a harder starting point for the proposed model in §6 than "some fields are missing" — it means the very idea of an inactive/sub-lot parcel *having* its own Grand-List attribution (value, owner, exemptions) is something the current pipeline has never had to support at scale, only for the TIF-town minority.

## 5. Limits of the current model (pre-redesign)

1. **The "Active parcel" is the *old* legal parcel definition** (contiguous land, common ownership — 32 V.S.A. §4152(a)(3) *before* Act 164) — not a "separate and sellable lot," which is what Act 164 now legally requires for mapping purposes starting April 1, 2028. Today's Active/Inactive split already gestures at this distinction (`PARENTSPAN` links an inactive sub-lot to its billing-active parent) but the *primary, published* layer is the aggregated/billing view, not the disaggregated/sellable-lot view.
2. **No dwelling-unit count anywhere in this pipeline** — not on `poly_parcels`, not on the intersection table, and not in the `GRANDLIST` field list in §3. This independently confirms the same gap flagged from the CAMA side in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) §7.1: Act 170's CY2027 "number of dwelling units" grand-list column requirement has **no existing source field anywhere in the current pipeline** — CAMA, Grand List, or GIS.
3. **No 3-way classification or floor-space proration** — `hsted_flv`/`nres_flv` on `GRANDLIST` is a binary homestead/nonhomestead split, matching MSOL CAMA's own binary `homestead` field. Act 170's 3-way classification (homestead / nonhomestead residential / nonhomestead nonresidential) with floor-area proration for mixed use has no analog today at any layer.
4. **`PARENTSPAN` and the condo-common-land SPAN convention are narrower, single-purpose precursors** to what a general `ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN` pair would need to do (§6) — they each solve one shape of the multi-record problem (horizontal aggregation, or condo common land specifically) rather than a unified structural model covering both horizontal and vertical multi-record conditions consistently.
5. **The GIS layer and the Grand List module track the same "inactive parcel points at its active parent" relationship using two different keys.** The GIS-side `poly_inactive.PARENTSPAN` field (§4) is SPAN-keyed; the Grand List module's own "Contiguous Parcel Information" panel (§1.5) is **`Parcel #`-keyed**, even though each parcel also independently carries its own SPAN. Any redesign that introduces a single `ADMINSPAN` field needs to reconcile this — it isn't simply exposing an existing SPAN-keyed relationship that already lives in NEMRC's software.
6. **Cross-town contiguous parcels (§1.5) aren't accounted for anywhere in this model.** NEMRC's own lister guidance explicitly asks listers to check for contiguity across town borders, but SPAN is town-scoped and each town's Grand List database is separate — it's unclear whether/how this is actually handled today, let alone in the proposed model.
7. **The Tax Department's own NEMRC Grand List export structurally lacks Active/Inactive parcel status for the vast majority of towns.** Confirmed directly via a real sample ([NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md) §7): of the six-file NEMRC-to-Tax-Department export family, only one file (`411TFP`, TIF-by-parcel) carries a Active/Inactive/Non-taxable status field (`PACTIVE`) — and that file exists **only for towns with a TIF district** (roughly 20-odd of Vermont's ~260 towns). For the remaining towns, none of `411_gl`, `411EXP`, or `411TOT` carries per-parcel active/inactive status at all. This means **VCGI's own separately, voluntarily collected parcel geometry (the published Active/Inactive GIS layers, §4) is currently the more complete statewide source of inactive-parcel awareness** — more complete than what the Tax Department itself receives via this channel for non-TIF towns. Since the Grand List module's own UI does track Active/Inactive status on every parcel regardless of TIF status (§1.5), the gap appears to be in what's *exported* to the Tax Department, not necessarily in what NEMRC's software tracks internally — but this affects how much the proposed `ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN` model (§6) can lean on the Tax Department's existing Grand List data pipeline versus needing new export fields from NEMRC specifically for this purpose. **Mild additional supporting evidence for "software limitation, not export limitation":** NEMRC's own Grand List module Reports Menu includes dedicated "Contiguous Parcels" and "Inactive Parcels" report options (§1.6) — not proof the underlying data could be exported in the 411 file family, but consistent with the module tracking this status as a reportable concept internally, not just something visible ad hoc in the UI. **Now independently confirmed at the published-product level, too** (§4): the live `poly_standardized_inactive` FeatureServer schema carries no Grand-List-derived fields whatsoever — no owner, value, exemption, or category data of any kind, only geometry and edit/source metadata — because there generally isn't equivalent Grand List data available to join against inactive parcels outside TIF towns. The gap isn't just in one file family; it's visible end-to-end, from the raw NEMRC export through to what VCGI actually publishes.
8. **Nothing in the current pipeline — SPAN, CAMA, the Grand List, or the GIS layer — has a mechanism for representing a single physical parcel that straddles a town line as one entity.** Confirmed via a real CAMA training example ([MSOL_AS_BUILT.md §10](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md#10-land-valuation-tables-and-the-land-in-two-towns-cross-town-blending-method), [§1.5](#15-the-grand-list-modules-own-parcelcontiguous-parcel-ui) above): when one deeded parcel's boundary crosses a town line, each town independently assesses only its own portion, under its own town-scoped SPAN, with no formal cross-reference between the two towns' separate records — the CAMA valuation math accounts for the neighboring town's acreage only as an input to a rate-lookup formula, not as a link to a shared underlying parcel. This is structurally rooted in SPAN's own town-scoping (§2) and the Parcel Data Standard's per-town delivery model (§4, `VTPARCELS_<town>_<YYYY>.gdb`) — not something the Act 164/170 redesign has caused, but something it will need to either accept as a permanent limitation or actually address, since Act 164's new "Parcel" (mapping/sellable-lot) entity is explicitly meant to reflect true physical/legal lot boundaries, which don't stop at town lines just because Vermont's administrative systems do.

## 6. Proposed future-state model (VCGI/Tax Dept/NEMRC workgroup, July 27, 2026)

*From an internal workgroup PDF ("Parcel Definition Workgroup — NEMRC Prep Diagrams"). This is a proposal under active discussion, not an adopted standard — treat field names/domains below as a working draft.*

### 6.1 Core idea: split "Parcel" from "Administrative Parcel"

- **Parcel** ("formerly Inactives") — an index of documented areas in land records; represents a **separate, sellable lot**, matching Act 164's new mapping-purpose parcel definition. VCGI's own read (independent of the workgroup PDF, per direct conversation) is that **elevating the existing "Inactive" layer to become the new primary/active mapping layer** is the most logical path to satisfying this definition, since `poly_inactive` already models sub-lot boundaries today — just not as the *primary* published layer.
- **Administrative Parcel** — contiguous ownership, used for tax billing, Current Use administration, etc. This is essentially today's "Active parcel" concept, renamed and formalized as explicitly *not* the mapping/sellable-lot entity anymore.

**Not yet addressed by this proposal, as currently documented:** a single physical parcel whose boundary straddles a town line (§5 item 8). Since SPAN itself is town-scoped and stays that way under this proposal — `ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN` are still SPAN-formatted, still implicitly one-town — a cross-town "Parcel" record has no obvious home in this model either, any more than it does today. Worth resolving explicitly given Act 164's mapping-purpose "Parcel" is meant to reflect real physical/legal boundaries (§7).

**On the `(or 0)*` and `(varies)*` annotations — confirmed, not speculative:** per direct input from Vermont Tax Department staff, municipal practice on stacked-unit acreage varies in the field: some towns divide the common land's acreage equally across all associated unit records, others assign the full acreage to the common-land record alone and list `0` on each associated unit record. Both practices exist today; there is no single enforced convention. VCGI's own [Act 68 of 2024 parcels report](https://github.com/VCGI/publications/blob/main/Act68_2024/Act68-2024-Parcels-VCGI_As_Submitted_20241212.md) flagged exactly this as an open item (Appendix 5, "Clarify Grand List vs. GIS Acreage Guidance Considerations") well before this workgroup — as of that report, stacked polygons accounted for 27,239 grand list records across 3,254 stacked polygons statewide, representing about 200,457 acres (~3.5% of all parcel acreage).

The `(varies)*` owner annotation reflects a real legal constraint, not just a data-entry inconsistency: under Vermont's Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act, **common-interest property cannot be separately listed on the grand list at all.** Per Tax Department guidance: *"By definition, there is common property involved in condominium ownership, and in property subject to the Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act (UCIOA) contained in Title 27A of the Vermont Statutes Annotated. The assessment of each unit must include the value of that unit's percentage of undivided interest in the common areas and common facilities. [27 V.S.A. § 1322](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/27/109/01322) and [27A V.S.A. §§ 1-101 et seq.](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/fullchapter/27A/001). For example, a 100-unit condominium where each unit is purchased without a divided interest in the common land, would be 100 parcels on the grand list, with each parcel having a 1% interest in the common property. The building, land and any common areas and facilities may not be separately listed."* This is consistent with the proposed model's `PARCEL`/`COMMON` record carrying `TAXBILL=NO` — it can exist as a **mapping** entity (since it needs to be represented in GIS) without existing as an independent **billing** entity (since UCIOA prohibits that on the grand list). See also [VCGI's documented current unlanded-structure mapping approaches](https://github.com/VCGI/publications/blob/main/Act68_2024/Act68-2024-Parcels-VCGI_As_Submitted_20241212.md#a331-current-unlanded-structure-and-common-interest-parcel-mapping-practices-in-vermont) (Stacked/Discrete/Distributed/Points, §4 above) for how this plays out geometrically today.

### 6.3 New field definitions (per the workgroup PDF)

**Structural/relational fields — addressing H.933:**

| Field | Purpose | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| `KIND` | Differentiates physical mapping entities from tax billing entities | `PARCEL`, `ADMINPARCL` |
| `TYPE` | Sub-type of the multi-record condition | `FULL`, `SINGLE`, `PARTSURFCE`, `PARTSTACKD`, `COMBINED`, `COMMON` (full domain as observed across the worked examples) |
| `ADMINSPAN` | Parent key for **horizontal** conditions — links contiguous sub-lots (`TAXBILL=NO`) to their aggregated billing record (`TAXBILL=YES`) | Valid SPAN |
| `GROUNDSPAN` | Foreign key for **vertical** conditions — links unlanded units (`TAXBILL=YES`) to their underlying common ground polygon (`TAXBILL=NO`) | Valid SPAN |
| `TAXBILL` | Boolean flag: does this record generate an actual tax bill (prevents double-billing) | `YES`, `NO` |
| `PARCLCOUNT` | Integer count of "separate and sellable" lots, for State funding calculations | Integer |

**`GROUNDSPAN` would be built from nothing, not adapted from an existing concept — now confirmed directly from the CAMA side, not just the GIS side.** [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md §11](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md#11-the-condominium-valuation-system--and-a-confirmed-concrete-gap-directly-relevant-to-groundspan) reviewed MicroSolve's actual condominium valuation system (a 2025 NEMRC training presentation, cross-checked against the real South Burlington condo extract) and found **no field, table, or record type anywhere that links a condo unit to its underlying common-element/ground parcel, or to any other unit in the same complex.** Each unit's own SPAN is fully independent; the one apparent grouping mechanism (a "Neighborhood Code") is confirmed to be a pure valuation-table lookup key with no parcel or legal significance. This matches, and now extends to the CAMA side, what §4 already established about the GIS layer's own `C-<town>-<sequence>` synthetic-SPAN convention being an Intersection-Table-only construct — together, this means `GROUNDSPAN` isn't a matter of exposing or renaming something that already exists in either system. It's new construction on both sides.

**`PARCLCOUNT`'s "State funding calculations" now has a confirmed referent:** per the Tax Department's policy advisor, Act 164's parcel-definition split (§4152(a)(3)) explicitly carves out "mapping and per parcel payments under subsections 4041a(a) and 5405(f)" as the purposes for which the sellable-lot definition applies. Those two statutes are real, existing per-parcel payment mechanisms: **32 V.S.A. §5405** (a per-parcel payment to towns, currently $1.00/parcel, for assisting PVR with the equalization study) and **32 V.S.A. §4041a** (two per-parcel reappraisal payment multipliers — grand list maintenance and reappraisal year, added by Act 170 Sec. 35). `PARCLCOUNT` is very likely meant to directly feed these two payment calculations, not a vague or hypothetical "funding" reference.

**Valuation & classification fields — addressing H.955** (extending the binary homestead split toward the 3-tier proportional system):

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `NRES_RES_FLV` | Splits out the Nonhomestead **Residential** Full Listed Value (from today's single `nres_flv`) |
| `NRES_NONRES_FLV` | Splits out the Nonhomestead **Nonresidential** Full Listed Value |
| `FLR_PCT_HS` | % of finished floor space allocated to Homestead |
| `FLR_PCT_NR` | % of finished floor space allocated to Nonhomestead Residential |
| `FLR_PCT_NN` | % of finished floor space allocated to Nonhomestead Nonresidential |

**`DWELLINGS` status — the legal definition is now settled; the sourcing mechanism and its interaction with the parcel redesign are not.** The Tax Department published its own guidance, ["Dwelling Unit Determination"](https://tax.vermont.gov/property/classification/dwelling-unit-determination) (updated August 13, 2026, [`reference/dwelling-unit-determination-aug-13-2026.md`](reference/dwelling-unit-determination-aug-13-2026.md); decision-tree graphic at [`reference/FL-1363 Dwelling Unit Determination.pdf`](<reference/FL-1363 Dwelling Unit Determination.pdf>)), which resolves what this section previously flagged as "still being worked out." A dwelling unit is now legally defined as a building or part of a building with its own separate entrance, designed/suitable for habitation (sleeping, cooking, sanitary facilities), and **fit for year-round habitation** — evidenced by a sufficient heating system, weatherization, usable year-round plumbing, and reasonable year-round access; local permitting/zoning is explicitly irrelevant. Camps and similar seasonal structures are explicitly **excluded** even if declared as a Homestead — the guidance is explicit that Homestead status and dwelling-unit status are legally independent determinations.

**This settles the definition but doesn't settle how it gets computed or recorded.** The guidance itself states: *"This field will originate from the town's Computer Assisted Mass Appraisal (CAMA) system, which will then be transmitted into the NEMRC Grand List system as part of the existing CAMA upload."* This is a **policy confirmation that CAMA, not the Grand List module, is the intended source** — resolving half of OQ-2 — but it raises two new problems, not previously visible:

1. **"The existing CAMA upload" is ambiguous between two already-documented, different channels**: the §5404(b) statutory CAMA extract (§1.4, submitted annually via Globalscape to PVR) and the vendor-agnostic **NEMRC Standard Import** (a fixed, 24-field CSV format any CAMA system can generate to feed the Grand List module directly — newly documented in [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md §12](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md#12-the-nemrc-standard-import--the-vendor-agnostic-path-from-any-cama-system-into-the-grand-list)). These are not the same pipeline, and neither currently has room for a dwelling-unit field without a schema change. Worth confirming directly which one the Tax Department means.
2. **The definition requires a year-round-habitability *determination*, not just a unit count.** None of the vendor CAMA fields already documented as dwelling-count candidates (Aumentum's `ResLivingUnits`, Catalis's `Rental Living Units`, VTPIE's never-built `NumberofDwellings`) capture heating/weatherization/access/plumbing adequacy — a lister judgment call, per the guidance's own words, based on "the overall facts and circumstances." Simply renaming an existing unit-count field to `UNITCNT`/`DWELLINGS` (the approach recommended in [VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md §4.4](VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md#44-buildingimprovement-detail-phase-1--carried-forward-directly-from-the-act-68-2024-proposal)) would not, on its own, satisfy this legal test — it counts units, not their year-round habitability.

**A previously unconsidered and significant conflict, worth raising directly, not just noting:** the guidance's own text states *"A 'parcel' means all contiguous land under the same ownership, together with all improvements on that land"* — this is **the old, pre-Act-164 parcel definition** (32 V.S.A. §4152(a)(3) as it read before Act 164), not the new "separate, sellable lot" definition the entire Parcel/Administrative Parcel redesign in this section is built around. Read plainly, this means dwelling units are meant to be counted per **Administrative Parcel** (`KIND=ADMINPARCL`, the billing entity), not per the new mapping-purpose `PARCEL`. That's a workable, sensible answer for the simple worked example in §6.2 (where `PARCEL` and `ADMINPARCL` carry identical `DWELLINGS` values 1:1) — but it's unclear what happens when a `TYPE=COMBINED` Administrative Parcel aggregates multiple underlying sellable `PARCEL` records: does `DWELLINGS` on the Administrative Parcel simply sum the constituent parcels' counts, and is there any expectation of a disaggregated per-`PARCEL` count at all, given the Tax Department's own guidance frames "parcel" using the contiguous-ownership (Administrative) concept exclusively? This wasn't addressed by either Act 170's dwelling-unit provision or the workgroup's parcel-redesign proposal, and the two efforts don't appear to have been explicitly reconciled with each other on this point. **A confirmed condo-specific wrinkle for the same definition:** MSOL's condo valuation system has a real "Lock-Off" concept — a single legal condo unit with an internal partition allowing part of it to be privately used or rented separately ([MSOL_AS_BUILT.md §11.2](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md#112-the-valuation-system-a-blank-slate-no-built-in-cost-tables)) — meaning one unit (one SPAN, one billing record) doesn't necessarily correspond to one habitability-determined dwelling unit under the new legal test either.

**`LOCALID` status — confirmed, not speculative:** `LOCALID` is an intentional general-purpose placeholder representing whatever municipality-specific identifier a town actually uses — typically a Parcel ID and/or Map ID, which sometimes both exist for the same parcel. Per VCGI: this representation has to persist in the model because municipalities continue to treat their own local identifiers as the "real" unique key, even though **SPAN is the actual statewide unique identifier**. This is worth remembering as a data-quality/adoption risk for the overall redesign, not just a naming choice — local-ID attachment at the municipal level is a real friction point independent of what the schema itself specifies.

`SPAN` and `OWNER1`/`LISTED_AC` (≈ `acresgl`) are not new — they already exist today on the GIS layer and `GRANDLIST` table respectively, and are simply carried through into the new model.

### 6.4 GIS implementation changes (as stated in the workgroup PDF)

- **Elevating "Inactives":** the legacy inactive-parcel geometry layer becomes the active, primary mapping layer representing all "sellable lots" per Act 164/H.933.
- **Retiring complex SQL joins:** embedding `ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN` directly in the Grand List means the multi-join `JoinGL2Parcels` stored procedure (§4) "can be drastically simplified or retired."
- **Update of the Parcel Data Standard, guidance, and templates** — v2.3 (§4) would need a substantial revision; possible new tooling, developed jointly with vendors. A preliminary, VCGI-authored starting point for the CAMA side of this now exists: [VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md](VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md), synthesizing all four vendor extracts, the NEMRC Grand List export, the confirmed published-layer schema, and this section's own proposed fields into one candidate schema.
- **CAMA data integration:** improved record identification/management is expected to permit a more reliable relationship with detailed CAMA property info — explicitly tied to **32 V.S.A. § 5404**, the existing statutory reporting requirement that is *also* the reason VCGI has been collecting the NEMRC/Aumentum/Vision/Catalis CAMA samples documented in [readme.md](readme.md) and [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) in the first place. **This connects the two initiatives:** the CAMA-schema documentation effort and this parcel/SPAN redesign aren't separate tracks — §5404 compliance is the throughline linking both.

### 6.5 Regional Assessment Districts and PVR's rulemaking mandate (Act 170 §3417)

*This section corrects a stale cross-reference: [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md)'s gap analysis said this content "lives in [SPAN_PARCEL_GRANDLIST_MODEL.md](SPAN_PARCEL_GRANDLIST_MODEL.md) §§6–7" — it hadn't actually been written up here until now.*

Act 170 creates a new 32 V.S.A. chapter 121, subchapter 1A (§§3415–3419), establishing **Regional Assessment Districts**. This is the one place in either act that names CAMA systems directly, and it's a **statutory mandate**, not a suggestion:

> §3415 (Legislative Intent): "...to create regional assessment districts so that: (1) properties on grand lists are regularly reappraised; **(2) property data collection is consistent and standardized across the State**; and (3) property valuation is conducted by trained and certified individuals and firms."

> §3417 (Standard Guidelines; Procedures; Rulemaking): "The Director of Property Valuation and Review shall establish standard guidelines and procedures, and may adopt rules, for regional assessment districts, including: (1) guidelines for contracting with third parties...; (2) **standards for the collection and recordation of parcel data**; (3) **requirements relating to information technology, including standards for data software contracts and computer-assisted mass appraisal systems**; and (4) standardized practices for a full reappraisal..."

**Why this matters more than any other single provision documented here:** §3417 gives the PVR Director explicit rulemaking authority over exactly the two things this entire documentation set is about — parcel data collection standards, and CAMA/IT software contract requirements. Everything in §6.1–6.4 (the proposed `KIND`/`TYPE`/`ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN`/`TAXBILL`/`PARCLCOUNT`/`DWELLINGS`/`FLR_PCT_*` fields) is a candidate *input* to that rulemaking, not a separate, informal side-conversation with NEMRC. This is the statutory hook that could turn this documentation effort into an actual adopted state standard rather than a bilateral vendor negotiation.

**Mechanics and timing:**
- Member municipalities of a regional assessment district must fully reappraise their grand lists every six years (§3416), and may jointly contract with third parties (CAMA vendors, appraisal firms) to do so.
- District boundaries are to align with school districts, each with a minimum of 10,000 parcels (Sec. 54); boundary recommendations are due to the Legislature by December 15, 2029.
- Districts commence operation **January 1, 2031**.
- New **Regional Assessment District Appeals Boards** (§§3418–3419) replace municipal Boards of Civil Authority for valuation appeals, with board seats scaling at one per 1,000 parcels (rounded up) in the municipality — taking over appeal jurisdiction **July 1, 2031**.
- All of this is contingent on the same foundation-formula trigger condition referenced elsewhere in Act 170 (2025 Acts & Resolves No. 73, as amended).

**Practical implication:** PVR's rulemaking authority under §3417 isn't itself gated to the 2031 district start date — there's no statutory reason the standards-setting couldn't begin sooner, and 2031 will arrive with towns needing a common CAMA/parcel-data standard to actually operate jointly across a multi-town district. That makes this section, and the redesign proposal in §6.1–6.4, directly relevant to PVR now, not just in 2031.

## 7. Open questions for the ongoing workgroup

*VCGI, the Tax Department, and NEMRC are already meeting on this — these are gaps identified while reconciling the workgroup's proposal against the actual current-state pipeline documented in §§3–4, not a suggestion that discussion hasn't started.*

1. **Where is `DWELLINGS` implemented, and what counts as a dwelling?** Confirmed (§6.3) that it exists at neither CAMA nor Grand List level today, and that the underlying definition of "dwelling unit" is still being worked out. Once that definition is settled: does NEMRC's Grand List module compute the rollup, does it require a new CAMA export field (e.g. summing MSOL's `EXP_SECTION.no_of_unit`, per [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) §7.1), or is it entered manually per parcel by listers? **Update from the second vendor documented (Aumentum ProVal, see [PROVAL_AS_BUILT.md](PROVAL_AS_BUILT.md) §4):** ProVal's `Valuation_History` table already carries a native, historically-tracked `ResLivingUnits` field that looks like a direct dwelling-count analog — and it went completely unused in VCGI's own independent dwelling-count methodology built for that vendor's demo viewer. Before deciding a `DWELLINGS` field needs to be invented from scratch industry-wide, it's worth checking each vendor for an existing native field like this one that may have simply gone unstandardized or unnoticed.
2. **Same sourcing question for `FLR_PCT_HS`/`FLR_PCT_NR`/`FLR_PCT_NN`.** The percentage-of-building-by-use mechanics already exist in MSOL CAMA's *commercial* silo (`EXP_OCCUPNCY.occ_perc`, per [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) §7.3) but not the residential silo, and nothing today maps any CAMA percentage breakdown onto these three specific classification buckets. Where is this computed, and does it require new CAMA export fields from all four VT CAMA vendors, not just NEMRC?
3. **Timing sequencing:** Act 164's parcel-definition change is effective 2028; Act 170's classification fields are effective 2029 (contingent) and regional districts 2031. Does elevating the Inactive layer to primary (§6.4) happen on the 2028 timeline independent of the classification-field work, or are they being implemented as one bundled release?
4. **Does the `e911addr` field on the current `GRANDLIST` table (§3) already solve — or partially solve — the CAMA-to-E911 address gap** flagged in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md) §7.2, or is it unreliably populated in practice?
5. **Is NEMRC's Grand List module (not just MSOL CAMA) prepared to originate/expose `ADMINSPAN`, `GROUNDSPAN`, `KIND`, and `TYPE`** — given NEMRC is the SPAN-issuing authority for essentially all VT towns regardless of CAMA vendor (§1), this is arguably a bigger ask of NEMRC's Grand List product than anything asked of MSOL CAMA specifically.
6. Per §6.4's connection to **32 V.S.A. § 5404**: this is directly the same statute behind VCGI's **current, active effort to formalize CAMA data transfer from all four VT vendors as-is** (building on the Act 68 of 2024 report's original recommendation to create a CAMA data standard and require standardized, regular vendor submittal). **Confirmed (VCGI, 2026-08-03): VCGI expects the Act 164/170 changes documented here will indeed impact the CAMA data standard, and by extension the extracts vendors submit to the State to remain in compliance with it** — not a separate, independent track. One explicit purpose of formalizing the as-is transfer first is to see exactly how (or whether) dwelling units are currently handled across vendors' existing data management, directly informing question 1 above.
7. **Stacked-unit acreage attribution (§6.2/§6.3) varies by town today** (equal division vs. full-on-common-record-with-zeros) — does the future-state model intend to standardize this practice, or continue accommodating both?
8. ~~What exactly flows back from CAMA to NEMRC's Grand List database?~~ **Largely resolved (§1.5).** Two distinct behaviors, not a contradiction: parcel splits/transfers require manual, dual entry in both systems (no auto-sync, per NEMRC's 2016 training material); but ordinary value/identifying-data updates on an already-existing parcel sync automatically, field by field, in both directions (NEMRC → MSOL: owner/address/911 data/tax map/property description/acres/sale data; MSOL → NEMRC: `cama_total`/`homestd_va`/`cama_site2` → Real/Homestead/Housesite), per a NEMRC document titled "Link between NEMRC and CAMA" — which also names the two live database tables (`LSPROP01`, `MAIN`) and describes an automatic "Change of Appraisal" flag and a documented reconciliation procedure for when sync fails. This is consistent with, and much more detailed than, the historical (2003) description of the same pattern cited in [MSOL_AS_BUILT.md](MSOL_AS_BUILT.md). **Still open:** whether new-parcel/SPAN requests ever originate in CAMA (the documented sync only covers existing, already-linked parcels); whether this exact mechanism is unchanged as of today, given the source document's own apparent ~2014 vintage; and whether Aumentum/ProVal, Vision, or Catalis/AssessPro have any comparable live sync with NEMRC, or rely solely on batch extracts (§1.4).
9. **Does VTPIE (§1.2) actually depend on NEMRC for SPAN?** Flagged with a "?" in VCGI's own working diagram — unconfirmed, not just unlikely.
10. ~~Geometry/Grand-List timing mismatch (§1.1): worth surfacing as a known data-quality consideration?~~ **Resolved (VCGI, 2026-08-03) — this is expected, not a defect.** Parcel geometry arrives from town-hired mapping vendors on a rolling basis, while the Grand List is joined only once per year — so a town's geometry can be newer than its most recently available Grand List record. Newer geometry is still joined via SPAN to the most recent Grand List record, where one exists as a `GLIST_SPAN`. This mismatch is an accepted, understood consequence of the two datasets' differing publishing workflows, not an anomaly needing investigation. See also item 20 below (the `YEAR`/`GLYEAR` and `TOWN`/`TNAME` field pairs, same underlying cause).
11. **Does Catalis AssessPro track a Vermont SPAN internally at all?** The one sample examined ([ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md](ASSESSPRO_AS_BUILT.md)) has no SPAN-equivalent field, in contrast to ProVal, which does (under a different name). Given the sample is in a generic third-party export format not specific to Vermont, this doesn't resolve whether AssessPro's actual database carries SPAN — worth a direct question to Catalis rather than assuming either answer from one non-VT-specific export. Per §1.4, this is now a **statutory-compliance** question, not just a data-completeness one: 32 V.S.A. §5404(b) requires the annual CAMA extract to identify parcels by the Director-prescribed numbering system (SPAN) — if Catalis's actual §5404(b) submission has no SPAN field, that's a compliance gap.
12. **Will Aumentum/ProVal, Vision, and Catalis/AssessPro use the same Globalscape FTP arrangement NEMRC uses** (§1.4), in their own vendor-specific folders, or a separate transfer mechanism per vendor? Unconfirmed as of this writing — worth resolving before assuming a uniform statewide submission channel exists.
13. **How would a `Parcel #`-keyed `ADMINSPAN` (or a SPAN-keyed one) actually get built from NEMRC's current Grand List module** (§1.5), given the existing "Contiguous Parcel Information" mechanism already tracks this relationship, just via a different key than the GIS layer's own `PARENTSPAN`?
14. **Does contiguous-parcel combination actually occur across town lines today** (§1.5/§5 item 6), given each town's Grand List database is separate and SPAN is town-scoped — or is the practice guidance about lister judgment only, with no cross-town software linkage actually possible?
15. **Could the existing `HS-122` and `TIF` tabs on the Grand List module's parcel record (§1.5) be extended** to carry a future dwelling-use attestation (Act 170) or classification data, rather than building an entirely new mechanism?
16. **Could non-TIF towns' NEMRC Grand List exports be extended to carry a `PACTIVE`-equivalent field?** Confirmed (§5 item 7, [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md) §7) that Active/Inactive/Non-taxable status is exported only for TIF-district towns today, even though the Grand List module's own UI tracks this status on every parcel regardless of TIF status (§1.5). Closing this export gap would reduce how much the `ADMINSPAN`/`GROUNDSPAN` model has to rely on VCGI's own voluntarily-submitted geometry as the source of truth for inactive-parcel status.
17. **What is the full scope of the transformation the Tax Department applies between NEMRC's raw Grand List export and what VCGI ultimately receives** ([NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md) §9)? Two changes are confirmed (exemption codes resolved to text, `locaprop` dropped) but the full extent is not documented anywhere accessible to VCGI.
18. **Does [32 V.S.A. §5412](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/32/135/05412) (PVR-applied education-only exemptions, newly surfaced in [NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md](NEMRC_GRANDLIST_EXPORT_AS_BUILT.md) §5) have any bearing on the proposed classification/exemption fields in §6.3**, given it describes an exemption category applied by PVR directly rather than sourced from any town-level Grand List field?
19. ~~Is `LOCAPROP` actually still populated on the published Active layer, or present-but-empty?~~ **Resolved (VCGI, 2026-08-03):** present in the schema, but empty for every record (§3).
20. ~~Are the `YEAR`/`GLYEAR` and `TOWN`/`TNAME` field pairs on the published Active layer (§3/§4) ever monitored for disagreement?~~ **Resolved (VCGI, 2026-08-03) — see item 10 above.** These fields can and do disagree for a given parcel, and that's expected: geometry and the Grand List are received/published on different schedules (rolling vs. annual), so newer geometry is routinely joined against the most recent available Grand List record rather than a same-vintage one.
21. **Why was `HSTED_TX` (raw `411_gl` field 28, taxable homestead value) dropped from the published Active layer**, when its full-value counterpart `HSTED_FLV` and several other similarly-derived value fields were kept? Worth confirming whether this was an intentional scoping decision or simply never carried through the transformation pipeline.
22. **Would Aumentum's `ResLivingUnits` and Catalis's `Rental Living Units` actually satisfy a canonical `UNITCNT`/`DWELLINGS` field, or do their existing definitions diverge from a plain "dwelling unit" count?** [VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md §4.4](VERMONT_CAMA_DATA_STANDARD_DRAFT.md#44-buildingimprovement-detail-phase-1--carried-forward-directly-from-the-act-68-2024-proposal) proposes adopting one of these vendor-native fields outright, plus VCGI's own 2024 `UnitCnt` proposal, rather than inventing a new field from scratch.
23. **Should the proposed Parcel/Administrative Parcel model explicitly address parcels that straddle a town line, and if so, how?** (§5 item 8, §6.1). Today this is handled entirely as a manual CAMA-side valuation workaround, town by town, with no formal linkage — is that an acceptable permanent state given Act 164's mapping-purpose "Parcel" is meant to reflect real physical/legal boundaries, or does a sellable-lot mapping layer need some way to represent a lot whose true boundary crosses a town line, independent of how each town separately bills it? Likely a policy question for the Tax Department/PVR and VCGI jointly, not something either can resolve unilaterally, and probably secondary to — but not unrelated to — the broader, longstanding case for a modern statewide municipal boundary survey.
